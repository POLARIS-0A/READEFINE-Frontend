Level 3 • Lesson 21

Fact and Opinion

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • 1. Differentiate between a fact and an opinion.
  • 2. Identify signal words commonly used in opinion statements.
  • 3. Evaluate statements to determine whether they can be verified with evidence.
  • 4. Understand the importance of distinguishing facts from opinions when reading.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

1. What Is a Fact?

A fact is a statement that can be proven true or verified using evidence, observation, research, or reliable records.

Example: "The Philippines is an archipelago."
Explanation: This statement is a fact because it can be verified through geographical information.

2. What Is an Opinion?

An opinion is a personal belief, feeling, judgment, or preference that may differ from one person to another.

Example: "The Philippines is the most beautiful country in the world."
Explanation: This is an opinion because beauty is based on personal preference.

3. Key Differences Between Fact and Opinion

  • Facts can be verified using evidence; opinions express personal beliefs or judgments.
  • Facts remain constant and supported by evidence; opinions vary based on experiences and preferences.

4. Signal Words of Opinion

Certain words and expressions indicate that a statement expresses an opinion:

• I believe • I think • In my opinion • Best • Worst • Beautiful • Should • Probably

5. Importance of Identifying Facts and Opinions

Distinguishing facts from opinions helps readers evaluate information carefully, avoid believing unsupported claims, make informed decisions, understand different perspectives, and recognize personal judgments in communication.

📌 Summary Examples

Example 1 (Fact)

"Water freezes at 0°C under standard atmospheric pressure."

Answer: Fact (Verified through scientific evidence)

Example 2 (Opinion)

"Mathematics is the most difficult subject."

Answer: Opinion (Based on individual learner experience)

Example 3 (Fact)

"Many students use smartphones for communication."

Answer: Fact (Supported by reliable data or observation)

Example 4 (Opinion)

"Students should have shorter school days."

Answer: Opinion (Expresses a recommendation or judgment)

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Facts can be verified through evidence.
  • Opinions express personal beliefs, feelings, or judgments.
  • Facts and opinions are fundamental types of statements.
  • Opinion signal words help identify personal viewpoints.
  • Identifying facts and opinions helps readers evaluate information critically.
