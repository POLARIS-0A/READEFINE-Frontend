Level 1 • Lesson 7

Subject and Predicate

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Identify the subject and predicate of a sentence.
  • Distinguish simple and complete subjects.
  • Distinguish simple and complete predicates.
  • Analyze sentence structure.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

A sentence is made up of important parts that work together to express a complete thought. Two of its main components are the subject and predicate.
Understanding these components helps learners construct grammatically correct sentences.

1. What Is a Subject?

The subject identifies the person, place, thing, or idea being discussed in a sentence.

Example: The young student answered the question.
• Complete subject: The young student
• Simple subject: student

2. What Is a Predicate?

The predicate tells what the subject does or what is said about the subject.

Example: The young student answered the question.
• Complete predicate: answered the question
• Simple predicate: answered

3. Simple Subject

The simple subject is the main noun or pronoun that the sentence is about.

Example: The hardworking teacher prepared the lesson.
• Simple subject: teacher

4. Complete Subject

The complete subject includes the simple subject and all the words that describe or identify it.

Example: The hardworking teacher prepared the lesson.
• Complete subject: The hardworking teacher

5. Simple Predicate

The simple predicate is the main verb or verb phrase that expresses what the subject does or is.

Example: The students have completed their assignments.
• Simple predicate: have completed

6. Complete Predicate

The complete predicate includes the verb and all the words that complete the information about the subject.

Example: The students have completed their assignments.
• Complete predicate: have completed their assignments

✨ More Examples

Example 1

The talented singer performed beautifully.

• Comp. Subject: The talented singer
• Simple Subject: singer
• Comp. Predicate: performed beautifully
• Simple Predicate: performed

Example 2

My older brother plays basketball every weekend.

• Comp. Subject: My older brother
• Simple Subject: brother
• Comp. Predicate: plays basketball every weekend
• Simple Predicate: plays

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • The subject tells who or what the sentence is about.
  • The predicate tells what the subject does or what is said about it.
  • The simple subject is the main noun or pronoun, while the complete subject includes its modifiers.
  • The simple predicate is the main verb, while the complete predicate includes the verb and additional information.
