Level 2 • Lesson 18

Writing Clear and Complete Sentences

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Explain what makes a sentence clear and complete.
  • Identify the basic parts of a complete sentence.
  • Recognize sentence fragments and run-on sentences.
  • Construct clear and grammatically complete sentences.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Sentences are important because they allow people to express complete thoughts and communicate information.
A clear and complete sentence helps readers understand the message without confusion. However, some sentences may be incomplete or contain too many connected ideas without proper punctuation.
Learning how to write complete sentences helps learners communicate more effectively in academic and everyday situations.

1. What Is a Sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought. It usually begins with a capital letter and ends with appropriate punctuation (period, question mark, or exclamation point).

Example: "The students are preparing for their examination." (Expresses a complete thought)

2. Subject & Predicate of a Sentence

Subject: Identifies the person, place, thing, or idea that the sentence is about.
Predicate: Tells what the subject does or what is said about the subject.

Sentence: "The young girl reads a book."
• Subject: The young girl
• Predicate: reads a book

3. Complete Sentence vs. Sentence Fragment

A complete sentence expresses a complete thought and contains both a subject and a predicate. A sentence fragment is a group of words that fails to express a complete thought because it is missing key parts.

✖ Fragment: "Because it was raining." (Incomplete thought)
✔ Complete: "Because it was raining, the students stayed inside."

✖ Fragment: "The boy in the classroom." (Missing predicate action)
✔ Complete: "The boy in the classroom is reading."

4. Run-On Sentences

A run-on sentence occurs when two or more independent clauses are joined incorrectly without appropriate punctuation or connecting words.

✖ Incorrect: "I studied for the examination I reviewed my notes."
✔ Correct Version 1: "I studied for the examination, and I reviewed my notes."
✔ Correct Version 2: "I studied for the examination. I reviewed my notes."

5. Writing Clear & Specific Sentences

A clear sentence communicates an idea directly without unnecessary fluff or confusing arrangements. It should identify specific persons, objects, and situations.

✖ Unclear: "The assignment that was given by the teacher yesterday needs to be done by the students."
✔ Clearer: "Students need to complete the assignment given yesterday."

✖ Vague: "She brought it."
✔ Specific: "Mia brought her notebook to class."

6. How to Write Clear and Complete Sentences

  • Identify the subject.
  • Make sure the sentence has a predicate.
  • Express a complete thought.
  • Use correct grammar and punctuation.
  • Avoid sentence fragments and run-on sentences.
  • Choose words that communicate the message directly.
📌 Summary Examples

Example 1: Complete Sentence

"The students completed their project."

Subject: The students | Predicate: completed their project.

Example 2: Fragment Corrected

"After finishing the activity." ➡️ "After finishing the activity, the students submitted their work."

Added independent clause to complete the thought.

Example 3: Run-On Corrected

"Maria likes reading she visits the library every week." ➡️ "...likes reading, so she visits..."

Joined correctly with comma and coordinating conjunction.

Example 4: Clear Expression

"The thing was placed there by him." ➡️ "Mark placed the book on the table."

Direct, active, and specific vocabulary.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought.
  • A sentence usually contains a subject and a predicate.
  • The subject tells who or what the sentence is about; the predicate tells what it does.
  • A complete sentence expresses a full thought; a fragment does not.
  • A run-on sentence joins independent clauses incorrectly.
  • Clear sentences communicate ideas directly and accurately.
