Summarizing and Paraphrasing

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Differentiate between summarizing and paraphrasing.
  • Identify the characteristics of a good summary.
  • Apply techniques for effective paraphrasing.
  • Avoid plagiarism by properly processing and acknowledging source ideas.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

A summary focuses on the central message and removes unnecessary details, repeated information, and minor examples.
Paraphrasing is expressing information from a text using different words and sentence structures while keeping its original meaning.

1. Characteristics of a Good Summary

  • Shorter: Concise and brief compared to the original text.
  • Accurate: Preserves the authentic original meaning.
  • Relevant: Focuses only on the most crucial ideas.
  • Clear & Objective: Easy to understand without personal bias.
Original: "Exercise helps improve physical fitness. It strengthens muscles, supports heart health, and increases endurance."
✔ Good Summary: "Exercise provides several benefits for physical health."

2. Steps in Summarizing

Read carefully ➡️ Identify main idea ➡️ Extract key supporting details ➡️ Remove fluff ➡️ Write concise version ➡️ Verify accuracy.

Original: "Trees are important to the environment. They provide oxygen, give shelter to animals, and help reduce the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere."
✔ Summary: "Trees help the environment by providing oxygen, supporting wildlife, and absorbing carbon dioxide."

3. Paraphrasing & Its Techniques

Unlike summarizing, paraphrasing keeps most detailed information while changing wording and sentence structure.

  • Using Synonyms: Replace words with contextually correct equivalents (e.g., "happy" ➡️ "joyful").
  • Changing Sentence Structure: Rearrange active/passive voice or sentence order.
  • Changing Word Forms: Transition words between parts of speech (e.g., "made a decision" ➡️ "decided").
  • Combining/Separating Ideas: Restructure long or short sentences cleanly.

4. Summarizing vs. Paraphrasing

Summarizing: Condenses overall central message into a much shorter form.
Paraphrasing: Restates a specific passage in full detail using fresh words.

5. Avoiding Plagiarism

Plagiarism is presenting another person's words or ideas as one's own. Paraphrasing does not replace the requirement to cite original sources when credit is required.

📌 Summary Examples

Example 1: Summarizing

"Online learning allows students to attend lessons using electronic devices..."

Summary: "Online learning allows students to study and access educational resources through electronic devices."

Example 2: Paraphrasing

"Time management helps students complete their tasks efficiently."

Paraphrase: "Students can finish their responsibilities more effectively when they manage their time properly."

Example 3: Comparison

"Healthy eating supports body development, gives energy, and maintains wellness."

• Summary: Healthy eating supports growth and overall health.
• Paraphrase: Eating nutritious food helps the body develop, provides energy, and supports a person's general well-being.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Summarizing presents the most important ideas in a shorter form.
  • Paraphrasing restates information using different words and structures.
  • Synonyms, sentence restructuring, and word-form changes aid paraphrasing.
  • Plagiarism is using others' ideas without proper attribution.
  • Original sources must be acknowledged whenever citations are required.
