Level 3 • Lesson 22

Identifying Bias and Point of View

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • 1. Define bias and point of view.
  • 2. Identify different types of bias in written information.
  • 3. Recognize the author's point of view.
  • 4. Explain how bias affects the presentation of information.
  • 5. Evaluate texts by considering the author's perspective and possible bias.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Writers communicate information based on their knowledge, experiences, beliefs, and perspectives. Sometimes, the way they present information may favor one side or show an unfair preference. This is known as bias.
Understanding bias and point of view helps readers examine how information is presented and recognize that different writers may describe the same event in different ways.

1. What Is Bias?

Bias is a preference, prejudice, or tendency toward a particular person, group, idea, or side that may influence how information is presented in a one-sided manner.

2. Types of Bias

  • Positive Bias: Focuses mainly on favorable qualities or achievements (e.g., mentioning only advantages in a review).
  • Negative Bias: Focuses mainly on unfavorable qualities or weaknesses while ignoring achievements.
  • Selection Bias: Occurs when examples or survey samples are chosen in a way that does not represent the entire situation.
  • Omission Bias: Happens when crucial information is left out, resulting in an incomplete presentation.

3. Point of View

Point of view refers to the position or perspective from which a writer presents information.

• First Person: Uses I, me, my, we, our.
• Second Person: Uses you, your.
• Third Person: Uses he, she, it, they, their.

4. Difference Between Bias and Point of View

Bias refers to a preference or tendency that may affect fairness. Point of view refers to the perspective from which information is told. A writer can have a point of view without showing unfair bias.

5. Importance of Recognizing Bias and POV

Helps readers understand perspectives, identify one-sided claims, consider varied opinions, avoid blind acceptance of texts, and develop critical thinking skills.

📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Positive Bias

"School A is the only school that provides excellent education."

Praises one school without evidence or considering others.

Example 2: Negative Bias

"The new school policy caused problems for every student."

Focuses solely on negative results with a broad unverified claim.

Example 3: 1st Person POV

"I believe that reading every day improves my vocabulary."

Uses the pronoun "I".

Example 4: 3rd Person POV

"She explained the importance of responsible internet use."

Uses the pronoun "she".

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Bias is a preference or tendency that can affect fairness.
  • Positive, negative, selection, and omission bias influence presentation.
  • Point of view refers to the narrative perspective used by a writer.
  • A point of view does not automatically mean a text is biased.
  • Recognizing bias helps readers evaluate information fairly.
