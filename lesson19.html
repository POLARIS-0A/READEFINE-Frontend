Level 2 • Lesson 19

Sentence Combining and Paragraph Development

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Explain what sentence combining means.
  • Identify different ways of combining sentences.
  • Use conjunctions and appropriate punctuation when combining ideas.
  • Develop a paragraph by connecting related sentences.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Writing becomes more organized when sentences are connected properly. Sometimes, two short sentences can be combined to create a smoother and more meaningful sentence.
Sentence combining helps writers avoid repetitive wording and improve the flow of their ideas.
Paragraph development also involves expanding a main idea by adding relevant explanations, examples, and supporting details.

1. What Is Sentence Combining?

Sentence combining is the process of joining two or more related sentences to create a clear and meaningful sentence. It helps writers express connected ideas without repeating unnecessary words.

Separate: Ana likes reading. Ana visits the library every week.
✔ Combined: "Ana likes reading, so she visits the library every week."

2. Combining Sentences Using Coordinating Conjunctions

Coordinating conjunctions connect words, phrases, or independent clauses of equal grammatical importance. Remember them through FANBOYS:

  • For – introduces a reason.
  • And – adds information.
  • Nor – connects negative ideas.
  • But – shows contrast.
  • Or – presents a choice.
  • Yet – shows contrast or an unexpected result.
  • So – shows a result.
Example: "Mark was tired, but he finished his assignment."

3. Combining Sentences Using Subordinating Conjunctions

Subordinating conjunctions connect a dependent clause to an independent clause, showing relationships like time, reason, condition, and contrast (e.g., because, although, when, while, since, if, before, after).

Example: "The students stayed inside because it was raining."

4. Combining Sentences Using Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns (e.g., who, whom, whose, which, that) introduce clauses that provide additional information about a noun.

Example: "The girl who won the competition is my classmate."

5. Avoiding Repetition

Sentence combining helps eliminate unnecessary repetition of subjects or ideas.

✖ Repetitive: Carlo is a responsible student. Carlo completes his assignments. Carlo studies every night.
✔ Combined: "Carlo is a responsible student who completes his assignments and studies every night."

6. What Is Paragraph Development & Ways to Develop It

Paragraph development expands a main idea using details, examples, facts, or explanations.

  • Giving Examples: Specific items or scenarios (e.g., apples, bananas, oranges).
  • Providing Facts: Information that can be checked or proven.
  • Giving Explanations: Explains how or why something happens.
  • Describing Details: Provides characteristics of the topic.

7. How to Develop a Clear Paragraph

Choose one main idea ➡️ Write a clear topic sentence ➡️ Add related supporting details ➡️ Combine related sentences ➡️ Use transition words ➡️ Arrange logically ➡️ End with a concluding sentence.

📌 Summary Examples

Example 1: Using "And"

"The students cleaned the room and arranged the chairs."

Combines two actions performed by the same subject.

Example 2: Using "Although"

"Although the examination was difficult, Mia completed it."

Shows contrast using a subordinating conjunction.

Example 3: Developing a Paragraph

"Time management helps students become organized. It allows them to plan their schedules and complete tasks on time. It also helps them identify their most important responsibilities. Because of these benefits, students should develop good time management habits."

Topic sentence expanded with clear supporting details and concluding thought.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Coordinating conjunctions connect ideas of equal grammatical importance.
  • FANBOYS stands for For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, and So.
  • Subordinating conjunctions connect dependent clauses to independent clauses.
  • Relative pronouns introduce clauses that provide information about nouns.
  • Examples, facts, explanations, and descriptions can develop a paragraph effectively.
