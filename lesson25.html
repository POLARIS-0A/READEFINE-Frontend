Level 3 • Lesson 25

Drawing Conclusions

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Define what a conclusion is in reading comprehension.
  • Explain the process of drawing conclusions using facts, details, and reasoning.
  • Differentiate between a logical conclusion and an unsupported guess.
  • Formulate well-supported conclusions based on given text evidence.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

When reading a report showing that many students improved their reading scores after regular practice, a reader may conclude that consistent reading practice can support reading development.

1. Drawing Conclusions

Drawing conclusions is the process of using facts, details, observations, and reasoning to arrive at a logical understanding.
It involves examining available information, identifying important details, connecting related ideas, applying logical reasoning, and forming a conclusion supported by evidence.

2. Evidence & Reasoning

• Evidence: Facts, details, examples, or observations that support a conclusion.
• Reasoning: The process of using logic to connect evidence to a conclusion.

3. Conclusion vs. Guess

  • A conclusion is grounded in concrete details and logical reasoning, allowing it to be explained through text evidence.
  • A guess is made without sufficient supporting information and relies primarily on random assumptions.
📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Weather Observation

"The ground is wet, people carry umbrellas, and dark clouds cover the sky."

Conclusion: It may have rained recently or be raining. (Based on multiple observations)

Example 2: Reading Fluency

"Maria practices reading aloud every evening. Her reading becomes smoother over time."

Conclusion: Regular practice may improve reading fluency.

Example 3: Library Visits

"A report shows that a school library increased its book collection and recorded more student visits."

Conclusion: The expanded book collection may have encouraged more students to visit the library.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • A conclusion is a judgment reached after examining information.
  • Drawing conclusions requires evidence and logical reasoning.
  • Important details help support a conclusion.
  • Conclusions are different from unsupported guesses.
  • Strong conclusions should be reasonable and supported by available information.
