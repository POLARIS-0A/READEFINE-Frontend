Level 4 • Lesson 33

Advanced Grammar and Sentence Correction

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

  • 1. Identify common grammatical errors in sentences.
  • 2. Recognize subject-verb agreement rules.
  • 3. Correct pronoun, tense, and modifier errors.
  • 4. Identify sentence fragments and run-on sentences.
  • 5. Apply grammar rules when editing written work.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Grammar provides rules that help people communicate ideas clearly and correctly. Advanced grammar focuses on recognizing common errors, refining sentence structure, and polishing academic or professional writing.

1. Key Grammar Rules

• Subject-Verb Agreement: Verbs must match their subjects in number and person (e.g., "She writes" vs. "They write").

• Verb Tense Consistency: Maintain a logical time reference (e.g., "Yesterday, she went to school.").

• Pronoun Agreement: Pronouns must correctly match the nouns they replace (e.g., "Maria forgot her notebook.").

• Misplaced Modifiers: Modifiers must be placed near the words they describe to prevent confusion.

2. Structural Sentence Errors

  • Sentence Fragments: Incomplete word groups lacking a subject, verb, or complete thought (e.g., "Because the students were late." → Corrected: "Because the students were late, the teacher extended the activity.").
  • Run-on Sentences: Independent clauses incorrectly joined without punctuation or proper conjunctions (e.g., "I finished my homework I watched television." → Corrected: "I finished my homework, and I watched television.").
📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Subject-Verb Agreement

Incorrect: "The students studies every night."
Correct: "The students study every night."

Plural subject "students" requires the plural verb "study".

Example 2: Verb Tense

Incorrect: "She visited her grandmother tomorrow."
Correct: "She will visit her grandmother tomorrow."

Time marker "tomorrow" signals future tense.

Example 3: Sentence Fragment

Incorrect: "Although he was prepared."
Correct: "Although he was prepared, he still reviewed his notes."

Adds an independent clause to complete the thought.

Example 4: Run-on Sentence

Incorrect: "The activity was difficult I asked for help."
Correct: "The activity was difficult, so I asked for help."

Uses comma + conjunction "so" to connect clauses.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Subject-verb agreement ensures subjects and verbs match in number and person.
  • Verb tense consistency maintains logical time continuity across sentences.
  • Pronouns must agree clearly in number and gender with their antecedents.
  • Modifiers should be placed directly next to the words they describe.
  • Fixing fragments and run-ons prevents ambiguity and improves writing quality.
