Level 4 • Lesson 38

Research and Information Literacy

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Define research and information literacy.
  • Identify the fundamental steps in conducting structured research.
  • Recognize the importance of evaluating information sources for accuracy and reliability.
  • Understand plagiarism and apply proper citation practices (APA, MLA, Chicago).
  • Demonstrate responsible information use in academic tasks.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Information literacy is the ability to recognize when information is needed and to locate, evaluate, and use it responsibly. In an era where information is instantly accessible online, these skills are crucial for academic success.

1. Research and Information Literacy

• Research: A systematic process of gathering, examining, and analyzing information to answer questions or investigate a subject.

• Information Literacy: The ability to recognize, locate, evaluate, organize, and use information effectively and responsibly.

2. Basic Steps in Conducting Research

  • Step 1: Identify the research topic.
  • Step 2: Formulate research questions.
  • Step 3: Gather information from appropriate sources.
  • Step 4: Evaluate sources for accuracy, authority, currency, and relevance.
  • Step 5: Organize and analyze information.
  • Step 6: Present findings through papers, reports, or presentations.

3. Avoiding Plagiarism and Using Citations

Plagiarism involves presenting someone else's words or ideas as one's own without proper credit. To maintain academic honesty, writers must use citations (such as APA, MLA, or Chicago styles) to acknowledge original sources.

📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Information Gathering

A student searches for climate change data using peer-reviewed scientific publications.

Systematic collection of reliable information.

Example 2: Plagiarism

Copying an entire web paragraph into an essay without any acknowledgment.

Using another author's work without giving proper credit.

Example 3: Proper Citation

Including the author's name and publication year next to referenced text.

Giving direct credit to the original source.

Example 4: Source Evaluation

Checking writer qualifications and comparing findings across reliable publications.

Practicing core information literacy skills.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Research is a systematic process of investigating questions and analyzing data.
  • Information literacy requires locating, evaluating, and using information responsibly.
  • Structured research flows from topic selection and questions to evaluation and presentation.
  • Plagiarism violates academic honesty; proper citations prevent it.
  • Responsible information use supports evidence-based and reliable conclusions.
