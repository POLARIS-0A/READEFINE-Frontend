Level 3 • Lesson 23

Evaluating Sources of Information

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Identify different sources of information (Newspapers, Websites, Journals, Interviews, Government publications).
  • Differentiate between primary and secondary sources.
  • Explain the characteristics of reliable information sources.
  • Evaluate online information for accuracy, authority, currency, relevance, and objectivity.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

1. Common Sources of Information

Information can be found in various platforms such as newspapers, websites, academic journals, interviews, and government publications.

2. Primary Sources

Primary sources provide original or firsthand information about an event, experience, or subject.

Examples: Diaries, letters, interviews, photographs, original documents, official records.
Example: A diary written by a person who experienced a historical event provides firsthand information from someone directly involved.

3. Secondary Sources

Secondary sources discuss, explain, interpret, or analyze information from primary sources or other materials.

Examples: Textbooks, biographies, research reviews, historical analyses, documentaries.
Example: A history textbook explaining a revolution interprets and organizes information from primary records.

4. Characteristics of Reliable Sources

  • Accuracy: Information is correct and supported by verifiable evidence.
  • Authority: The author or organization has relevant expertise or credentials.
  • Currency: Information is updated appropriately for current relevance.
  • Relevance: Information directly relates to the topic being studied.
  • Objectivity: Information is presented fairly and free from unnecessary bias.

5. Steps in Evaluating Online Information

  1. Check the author's name and qualifications.
  2. Examine the publishing website or organization.
  3. Look for supporting evidence and references.
  4. Check the publication or update date.
  5. Cross-check information with other reliable sources.
📌 Summary Examples

Example 1: Primary Source

"A signed letter written by a historical leader."

Answer: Primary Source (Original document from the author)

Example 2: Secondary Source

"A textbook discussing the life of a historical leader."

Answer: Secondary Source (Explains and interprets primary data)

Example 3: Potentially Reliable

"A government website publishes updated population statistics."

Official government data, but dates and details should still be checked.

Example 4: Questionable Source

"An anonymous social media post makes a health-related claim without evidence."

Lacks verifiable authorship and supporting proof.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Sources of information provide facts, ideas, and evidence.
  • Primary sources contain original or firsthand information.
  • Secondary sources interpret or analyze existing information.
  • Reliable sources demonstrate accuracy, authority, currency, relevance, and objectivity.
  • Information should always be checked and verified before being used or shared.
