Level 1 • Lesson 9

Sentence Types and Functions

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Identify the four types of sentences according to function.
  • Explain the purpose of each sentence type.
  • Recognize appropriate punctuation for each type.
  • Construct different types of sentences.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

People use sentences for different purposes. Sometimes they share information, ask questions, give instructions, or express strong emotions.
Understanding sentence types helps learners communicate their intentions clearly.

1. Declarative Sentence

A declarative sentence makes a statement or gives information. It usually ends with a period.

Examples:
• I am a college student.
• Reading improves vocabulary.
• The library closes at five o'clock.

2. Interrogative Sentence

An interrogative sentence asks a question. It ends with a question mark.

Examples:
• What time is the examination?
• Where did you put your notebook?
• Have you finished your assignment?

3. Imperative Sentence

An imperative sentence gives a command, request, instruction, or direction. It usually ends with a period, but may end with an exclamation point when strong emphasis is intended. The subject "you" is usually understood rather than directly written.

Examples:
• Please open your book.
• Submit your assignment tomorrow.
• Be careful!

4. Exclamatory Sentence

An exclamatory sentence expresses strong emotions such as excitement, surprise, or frustration. It ends with an exclamation point.

Examples:
• What a wonderful performance!
• I cannot believe we won!
• That is incredible!

5. Importance of Sentence Types

  • Declarative sentences communicate information.
  • Interrogative sentences gather information.
  • Imperative sentences communicate instructions or requests.
  • Exclamatory sentences express emotions.

🔑 Key Takeaways

There are four basic sentence types according to function:

  • 1. Declarative – makes a statement.
  • 2. Interrogative – asks a question.
  • 3. Imperative – gives a command or request.
  • 4. Exclamatory – expresses strong emotion.
