Level 4 • Lesson 34

Writing Coherent and Unified Paragraphs

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

  • 1. Define coherence and unity in paragraph writing.
  • 2. Identify the essential parts of a well-developed paragraph.
  • 3. Recognize the importance of logical organization.
  • 4. Use transitions and appropriate connections between ideas.
  • 5. Write paragraphs with clear and connected sentences.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

A paragraph is a group of related sentences developing one central idea. However, a good paragraph requires more than putting random sentences together; its ideas must be logically organized and connected smoothly through unity and coherence.

1. Paragraph Unity and Coherence

• Unity: Means all sentences in a paragraph directly support one central idea without unrelated details.

• Coherence: Refers to the logical and smooth connection of ideas so readers can follow how one sentence relates to another.

2. Essential Parts of a Paragraph

  • Topic Sentence: Introduces the main idea of the paragraph.
  • Supporting Sentences: Explain, describe, or provide evidence related to the topic sentence.
  • Concluding Sentence: Provides closure by summarizing or reinforcing the main idea.

3. Transition Words and Phrases

Transitions connect ideas and ensure smooth paragraph flow:
• Addition: Furthermore, moreover, also, in addition.
• Contrast: However, although, on the other hand.
• Cause and Effect: Therefore, consequently, as a result.
• Sequence: First, next, afterward, finally.
• Example: For example, for instance, such as.

📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Unified Paragraph

"Reading is an important learning habit. It helps students develop vocabulary and comprehension. Therefore, reading supports academic development."

Every sentence focuses strictly on the benefits of reading.

Example 2: Lack of Unity

"Reading improves vocabulary. It helps students understand texts. My favorite food is pizza. Reading also develops knowledge."

The sentence about pizza is entirely unrelated.

Example 3: Cause-and-Effect Transition

"She studied regularly. Therefore, she performed well on the examination."

Connects study habits directly to the test outcome.

Example 4: Topic Sentence

"Technology has changed the way people communicate."

Introduces the central idea of the paragraph.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Unity ensures all sentences within a paragraph support a single central idea.
  • Coherence links ideas logically and smoothly for optimal readability.
  • Topic sentences establish the main focus; supporting sentences elaborate on it.
  • Concluding sentences tie thoughts together and wrap up the paragraph.
  • Transition words guide readers through shifts in thought, addition, or contrast.
