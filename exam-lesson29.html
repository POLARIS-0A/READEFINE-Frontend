📝 Quiz 29: Informational and Argumentative Texts

Complete all test items below based on Lesson 29.

Part I. Identification (10 points)

Directions: Identify the term being described.

1. A type of text that provides facts and explanations about a topic.

2. A type of text that presents a position supported by reasons and evidence.

3. The main position that a writer wants readers to accept.

4. Explanations that show why a writer supports a claim.

5. Facts, statistics, and examples used to support an argument.

6. An opposing viewpoint that challenges a writer's claim.

7. A response that explains why the writer's position remains reasonable.

8. The final part of an argumentative text that reinforces the main position.

9. The main purpose of informational writing.

10. The main purpose of argumentative writing.

Part II. Multiple Choice (5 points)

Directions: Choose the letter of the correct answer.

1. Which is an example of informational writing?

2. Which is an example of argumentative writing?

3. What is the purpose of evidence in an argumentative text?

4. What is a counterargument?

5. What is the main difference between informational and argumentative texts?

Part III. Essay (5 points)

Explain the difference between informational and argumentative texts. Give one example of each and describe their purposes.
