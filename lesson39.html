Level 4 • Lesson 39

Effective Written and Verbal Communication

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Define written and verbal communication.
  • Identify the essential elements of effective communication (Sender, Message, Channel, Receiver, Feedback).
  • Recognize the importance of audience, purpose, and context.
  • Practice active listening strategies.
  • Apply clear, respectful, and effective communication skills.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Communication allows people to share information, express thoughts, and build relationships through writing, speaking, listening, and nonverbal expressions. Effective communication ensures messages are clear, appropriate, and suitable for the audience.

1. Written vs. Verbal Communication

• Written Communication: Sharing information through written words and symbols (e.g., emails, reports, essays, text messages).

• Verbal Communication: Sharing information through spoken words (e.g., conversations, presentations, speeches, meetings).

2. Elements and Core Considerations

  • Elements: Sender, Message, Channel, Receiver, and Feedback.
  • Key Factors: Audience (who receives it), Purpose (why it is sent), and Context (the situation or environment).

3. Active Listening

Active listening means giving full attention to the speaker, avoiding interruptions, asking relevant questions, and confirming understanding to ensure successful message exchange.

📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Written Communication

A student writes a formal email requesting information about an upcoming exam.

Information communicated through written text.

Example 2: Verbal Communication

A student explains group activity results during a classroom presentation.

Information delivered via spoken language.

Example 3: Active Listening

Listening closely, asking questions, and repeating instructions during a meeting.

Demonstrates total engagement and comprehension.

Example 4: Audience Awareness

Using simpler language when explaining a difficult concept to younger learners.

Tailoring delivery to match the audience's needs.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Written communication transmits information via text and written symbols.
  • Verbal communication relies on spoken words and interactive dialogue.
  • Effective communication involves a sender, message, channel, receiver, and feedback.
  • Audience, purpose, and context shape how messages should be framed.
  • Clarity, courtesy, correctness, and active listening drive successful interactions.
