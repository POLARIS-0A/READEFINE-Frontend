Level 4 • Lesson 32

Complex Sentence Structures

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

  • 1. Define clauses and complex sentence structures.
  • 2. Differentiate independent and dependent clauses.
  • 3. Identify simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences.
  • 4. Use coordinating and subordinating conjunctions correctly.
  • 5. Construct sentences with different structures.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Sentences form the foundation of written and spoken communication. Understanding sentence structures helps writers connect thoughts, explain conditions, and express complex relationships between ideas.

1. Clauses

• Independent Clause: Expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a sentence.
Example: "She finished her assignment."

• Dependent Clause: Contains a subject and verb but does not express a complete thought by itself.
Example: "Because she finished her assignment..."

2. Four Sentence Structures

  • Simple Sentence: Contains one independent clause (e.g., "The students studied.").
  • Compound Sentence: Contains two or more independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions—FANBOYS (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So).
    Example: "She studied hard, and she passed the examination."
  • Complex Sentence: Contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause joined by subordinating conjunctions (e.g., Because, Although, Since, When, While, If).
    Example: "She passed the examination because she studied hard."
  • Compound-Complex Sentence: Contains at least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause.
    Example: "Although it was raining, the students attended class, and their teacher continued the lesson."
📌 Practical Examples

Example 1: Simple

"The baby slept."

Contains one independent clause.

Example 2: Compound

"I wanted to study, but my internet connection stopped working."

Two independent clauses joined by "but".

Example 3: Complex

"Although she was nervous, she delivered her speech confidently."

One dependent clause and one independent clause.

Example 4: Compound-Complex

"After the class ended, the students packed their things, and the teacher arranged the chairs."

One dependent clause and two independent clauses.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • A clause always contains a subject and a verb.
  • Independent clauses express complete thoughts; dependent clauses do not.
  • Simple sentences contain a single independent clause.
  • Compound and complex sentences connect related ideas using conjunctions (FANBOYS & subordinators).
  • Compound-complex sentences combine multiple independent clauses with dependent clauses.
