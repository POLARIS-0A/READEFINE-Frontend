Level 3 • Lesson 24

Understanding Tone and Author's Purpose

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Define tone and author's purpose.
  • Identify different types of tone in written texts.
  • Recognize the main purposes of writing.
  • Examine word choice to understand a writer's tone.
  • Explain how tone and purpose affect a reader's understanding.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Every written text communicates a message in a particular way. Some writers aim to inform readers, while others want to entertain, persuade, or explain something. The words and expressions they use also create a certain feeling or attitude.

Understanding tone and author's purpose allows readers to recognize not only what a text says but also how and why the writer communicates the message.

1. Tone

Tone refers to the writer's attitude toward the subject, audience, or situation as expressed through word choice and writing style.

  • Formal: Uses professional and respectful language.
  • Informal: Uses casual and conversational language.
  • Serious: Communicates an important or thoughtful message.
  • Humorous: Creates amusement or laughter.
  • Critical: Expresses judgment or points out weaknesses.
  • Optimistic: Shows hope or confidence.
  • Pessimistic: Shows doubt or lack of hope.
  • Sympathetic: Shows understanding and concern.

2. Author's Purpose

Author's purpose refers to the main reason a writer creates a text.

• To Inform: The writer provides facts, explanations, or knowledge.
• To Persuade: The writer attempts to influence beliefs, opinions, or actions.
• To Entertain: The writer aims to provide enjoyment through stories or humor.
• To Explain: The writer describes how or why something happens or works.

3. Tone vs. Author's Purpose

Tone refers to the writer's attitude and answers: "How does the writer sound?"
Author's Purpose refers to the writer's reason for writing and answers: "Why did the writer write this?"

4. Identifying Tone

Readers can identify tone by examining word choice, sentence structure, expressions, descriptions, and the overall message.

5. Importance of Understanding Tone and Purpose

It helps readers recognize intention, understand the emotional message, interpret information accurately, identify persuasive language, and respond appropriately.

📌 Summary Examples

Example 1: Tone

"Please submit your completed requirements before the scheduled deadline."

Answer: Formal tone (Uses professional and respectful language).

Example 2: Tone

"Wow! That was the funniest performance I have ever watched!"

Answer: Humorous or excited tone (Shows amusement).

Example 3: Purpose

"Regular exercise supports physical health and overall well-being."

Answer: To inform (Provides facts about exercise).

Example 4: Purpose

"Join the reading program today and discover the benefits of reading!"

Answer: To persuade (Encourages readers to act).

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Tone is the writer's attitude toward the subject or audience.
  • Author's purpose is the reason a text is written.
  • Tone can be formal, informal, serious, humorous, and others.
  • Common purposes include informing, persuading, entertaining, and explaining.
  • Word choice and writing style help readers identify tone and purpose.
