Level 2 • Lesson 11

Reading Comprehension Strategies

← Back to Lessons
🎯 Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  • Define reading comprehension.
  • Identify different reading comprehension strategies.
  • Explain how reading strategies help readers understand a text.
  • Apply appropriate reading strategies when reading different texts.
💡 Introduction & Discussion

Reading is not only about recognizing words and reading sentences. It also involves understanding the meaning and information presented in a text.
Reading comprehension is the ability to understand, interpret, and remember what has been read.
Using reading comprehension strategies helps learners become better readers because these strategies guide them in understanding information, identifying important ideas, and remembering details.

1. What Is Reading Comprehension?

Reading comprehension is the ability to understand the meaning of written words, sentences, and paragraphs. It allows readers to understand what the author wants to communicate.

Sentence: Maria brought an umbrella because dark clouds covered the sky.
Understanding the sentence means recognizing that Maria expected rain, so she brought an umbrella.

2. Previewing

Previewing is a reading strategy that involves looking at a text before reading it completely. Readers examine the title, headings, pictures, and other important features to get an idea of what the text is about.

Example: Before reading an article titled "The Importance of Exercise," a learner looks at its headings and pictures to understand its possible topic.

3. Predicting

Predicting means making an educated guess about what may happen or what information may appear in a text based on title, pictures, and earlier clues.

Title: "A Day Without Electricity"
Prediction: The story may discuss the difficulties people experience when there is no electricity.

4. Skimming

Skimming is reading a text quickly to get its general idea or main point by focusing on titles, headings, topic sentences, and key words instead of reading every word.

Example: A student quickly reads the headings and first sentences of an article to understand its general topic.

5. Scanning

Scanning is reading quickly to find specific information, such as a name, date, number, or particular word without reading every sentence.

Example: A learner looks through a school announcement to find the date of an upcoming examination.

6. Asking Questions

Asking questions is a strategy that helps readers check their understanding before, during, and after reading.

  • What is the text about?
  • Who are the main characters?
  • Why did the event happen?
  • What lesson can be learned from the text?

7. Visualizing

Visualizing means creating mental pictures of the people, places, events, or situations described in a text.

Sentence: The children played happily under the tall trees while the cool wind blew.
A reader imagines children playing outside under trees with a gentle breeze.

8. Making Connections

Making connections means relating what is read to personal experiences, other texts, or events in the world.

  • Text-to-Self: Connecting the text to personal experiences.
  • Text-to-Text: Connecting the text to another story or reading material.
  • Text-to-World: Connecting the text to real-life events or situations.

9. Summarizing

Summarizing means giving a shorter version of a text by including only its most important ideas.

Original: A student studies every night, completes assignments, and reviews lessons to prepare for an examination.
Summary: The student prepares for the examination by studying regularly.

10. Monitoring Understanding

Monitoring understanding means checking whether the information being read is clear and understandable, and rereading or using context clues when confused.

📌 Summary Examples

Example 1: Skimming

Reading titles and first sentences of "Healthy Eating Habits".

Purpose: Get the general idea.

Example 2: Scanning

Looking through an announcement for an exam date.

Purpose: Locate specific info.

Example 3: Predicting

Guessing "The Lost Puppy" is about a puppy finding its owner.

Purpose: Make an educated guess.

Example 4: Visualizing

Imagining a golden sun disappearing behind mountains.

Purpose: Create mental pictures.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Reading comprehension means understanding what you read.
  • Previewing helps readers prepare before reading.
  • Active reading helps improve understanding.
  • Asking questions helps identify important information.
  • Summarizing helps remember the main points.
